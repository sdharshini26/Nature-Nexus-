Nature Nexus — Technical Design Document

1. Project Overview

Nature Nexus is a concept-stage sustainable packaging project focused on developing a compostable snack packaging pouch with separate seed strips that can be planted after use.

2. Design Objectives

- Explore alternatives to conventional packaging materials.
- Develop a proposed kraft-paper-based packaging structure.
- Incorporate a protective barrier layer to improve packaging performance.
- Attach viable seeds to separate strips for planting after use.
- Investigate material performance, seed viability, and compostability through future testing.

3. Proposed Material Composition

Material| Proposed Function
Kraft paper| Base material for the packaging pouch
Starch-based material| Component of the proposed barrier layer
Chitosan| Component of the protective barrier coating
Sorbitol| Plasticiser intended to help reduce brittleness
Natural wax| Proposed inner layer for moisture resistance
Guar gum| Adhesive for attaching seeds to seed strips
Cellulose paper or seed paper| Carrier for the seed strips
Viable seeds| Intended for planting after package use
Water| Hydration medium for preparing the guar-gum adhesive

The final formulation and compatibility of these materials require testing.

4. Proposed Packaging Structure

The design consists of the following elements:

1. Outer pouch: Kraft paper forms the main packaging structure.
2. Barrier layer: A proposed starch-based and chitosan coating containing sorbitol is investigated for protective performance.
3. Inner layer: Natural wax is proposed to improve moisture resistance.
4. Seed strips: Separate cellulose-paper or seed-paper strips carry viable seeds attached using guar-gum adhesive.
5. Seed placement: Seed strips are intended to remain outside the sealed snack chamber, along designated top and bottom edges.

The proposed structure must be evaluated to confirm its suitability for snack packaging.

5. Seed Strip Design

- Use one selected seed type per prototype batch.
- Attach seeds to cellulose paper or seed paper using guar-gum adhesive.
- Keep the seed strips separate from the food-contact area.
- Protect the seed strips from moisture during storage.
- Include a designated cut line to indicate where users can separate the strips for planting.

Potential seed choices include tulsi, marigold, fenugreek, and coriander. The final choice will depend on seed quality, storage stability, and germination testing.

6. Proposed Manufacturing Workflow

1. Prepare the kraft-paper pouch structure.
2. Develop and apply the proposed barrier-layer formulation.
3. Evaluate the natural-wax inner layer.
4. Prepare guar-gum adhesive formulations using different concentrations.
5. Attach the selected seeds to cellulose-paper or seed-paper strips.
6. Dry and store the seed strips under controlled conditions.
7. Integrate the seed strips into designated pouch edges without contacting the snack.
8. Inspect the assembled package before performance testing.

This workflow describes the proposed process; manufacturing trials have not yet been confirmed.

7. Testing and Validation Plan

The following tests are proposed for future evaluation:

- Adhesion test: Assess whether the seed strips remain attached during handling.
- Handling test: Check for seed displacement, strip detachment, or package damage.
- Moisture-resistance test: Evaluate the package under controlled humidity conditions.
- Water-release test: Observe how the seed-strip carrier softens and releases seeds when wetted.
- Germination test: Compare adhesive-treated seeds with untreated control seeds.
- Shelf-life test: Evaluate adhesion and seed viability over defined storage periods.
- Packaging performance test: Assess barrier performance, seal strength, and suitability for the intended snack.
- Compostability assessment: Evaluate the complete packaging system under appropriate test conditions.

No test results or performance claims are established at this stage.

8. Safety and Sustainability Considerations

- Assess food-contact materials for compliance with applicable requirements.
- Keep seed adhesives separate from the food-contact surface unless their suitability is established.
- Verify that coatings, adhesives, inks, and other package components are compatible with the intended end-of-life disposal method.
- Confirm seed viability after packaging and storage.
- Avoid claiming that the complete package is biodegradable, compostable, or plantable until appropriate evidence is available.

9. Current Project Status

Stage: Product design and concept development.

The packaging structure and material selection are proposed. Physical prototyping, material-performance evaluation, and seed-germination testing remain future work.

10. Future Development

- Develop a physical packaging prototype.
- Optimise the barrier coating and adhesive formulations.
- Evaluate moisture resistance and mechanical strength.
- Validate seed-strip adhesion and germination.
- Assess the complete package for compostability.
- Refine the design based on experimental results.

11. Conclusion

Nature Nexus explores a kraft-paper-based snack pouch with proposed protective layers and separate seed strips. The project aims to combine packaging functionality with a potential planting opportunity after use. Further prototyping and testing are required to establish material suitability, safety, and environmental performance.
